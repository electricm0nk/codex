Core Premise

NoDA built itself when nobody wanted it. Now reconstruction has made its independence valuable enough to steal.

Setting notes

The North Docks Arts District is an independent arts and industrial neighborhood reclaimed after the Fourth Corporate War by artists, refugees, merchants, Techs, Rockerboys, Nomads, squatters, and small-time operators. They restored their own utilities, defended their own streets, and built a culture from what the city abandoned.

The rebuilt monorail and expanding Upper Marina have turned that success into a target. Corporations now want to purchase the district, trademark its identity, sanitize its dangers, and resell an approved version of authenticity to people who could never have survived its lean years.

Name and History

North Docks Arts District is a practical name that became a declaration of ownership by the people who rebuilt it.

Setting notes

Before the War, the North Docks were warehouses, factories, worker housing, freight lines, and cheap commercial blocks. When the port economy collapsed, official maps treated the district as a dead zone.

Artists and workers moved into the shells. Techs restored machinery, Rockerboys filled the mills with sound, Nomads reopened freight routes, and families turned boarding houses into durable communities. NoDA became a name outsiders recognized only after locals had already made it real.

Neighborhood Compact

A voluntary code holds NoDA together, although age, debt, fear, and corporate offers are pulling it apart.

Setting notes

Members oppose chain businesses, offer property locally before selling outside, resist predatory evictions, contribute to utilities and security, and settle disputes inside the neighborhood whenever possible. It is not a government and has no police power. Its strength comes from reputation, mutual dependence, and the willingness to show up.

The Compact is breaking slowly. Some owners are tired, some need money, some want safe streets, and some believe Palisade’s promises are the only path to survival.

Setting notes

The plan advertises curated street art, sponsored venues, luxury conapts, private monorail access, branded Night Markets, and twenty-four-hour security. Many promised improvements are real, which is why the offer divides reasonable people.
