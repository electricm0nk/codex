# GE-09 Risks and Open Questions

## Active blockers
- GE-05 parity closure is still the next mandatory proof burden, so GE-06 remains explicitly capped at `computed-but-not-oracle-checked` rather than `pilot-viable`.
- GE-08 is planning-ready but still has no active code-authorizing handoff or settled contributor workflow, so contribution and package-lifecycle policy must remain provisional.

## Primary risks

### 1. Premature compatibility inflation
Risk: the program may make package or scope claims broader than the evidence actually supports.

Why it matters:
- this would recreate the opacity GE-00 is trying to kill
- it would turn the coverage dashboard into theater instead of control

### 2. Expansion by enthusiasm
Risk: once the pilot begins to feel real, new books or token families may be chosen by excitement instead of migration-control value.

Why it matters:
- it breaks the quality-gate doctrine
- it makes the roadmap look busy while weakening truth

### 3. Pilot-bounded evidence being mistaken for a global roadmap
Risk: the current GE-01 matrix and unsupported-token ledger could be over-read as if they already justify a fully detailed post-pilot expansion backlog.

Why it matters:
- the current evidence is still pilot-bounded
- counterfeit backlog precision would hide where the program still lacks grounded scope data

### 4. Missing authoring execution posture
Risk: release, compatibility, and contribution policy may be written as if authored packages already have a grounded lifecycle just because GE-08 now has a source STC.

Why it matters:
- GE-08 is real, but still planning-only
- contribution policy without narrower authoring truth becomes fantasy process

### 5. Release authority ambiguity
Risk: future sessions may assume that a package can be released once tests pass, without a clear authority model.

Why it matters:
- release is a different class of work than coding
- absent authority, the program will optimize for closure rather than survivable truth

### 6. Legal and licensing uncertainty
Risk: transformed, imported, or distributed package content may carry constraints that change what can be released or shared.

Why it matters:
- release posture could be technically ready but still legally blocked
- public compatibility language may need to remain narrower than internal use

### 7. Dashboard theater
Risk: the future dashboard may display categories and colors that imply coverage without tying them to real artifacts and review triggers.

Why it matters:
- a pretty dashboard can hide weak evidence better than no dashboard at all

## Open questions
- What exact parity threshold should promote a candidate band from hold to eligible broadening?
- Which known-gap classes are acceptable for a controlled preview versus a broader compatibility claim?
- What package-versioning and migration guarantees can honestly be made before authoring posture is narrower than today's GE-08 planning boundary?
- Who or what holds release authority once releases become real?
- What decision surface should own package signing or trust-network policy, if that becomes necessary?
- What legal or licensing constraints limit transformed or distributed content packages?
- What cross-platform formats matter first for internal pilot distribution versus broader release posture?
- When does contribution intake become necessary, and what is the minimum review gate set?
- What compatibility promise, if any, can be made for packages authored outside the pilot's original source path?

## Review triggers
Reopen this STC aggressively when any of the following happens:
- a GE-05 parity artifact changes the current gap class or raises the evidence ceiling
- GE-06 publishes a stronger or weaker propagated posture for the pilot or adjacent scope
- GE-08 creates a narrower readiness closure or decision surface for authoring/contribution workflow
- a doctrine decision sets release authority, signing, or intentional divergence policy
- the team proposes package publication, public sharing, or broader contributor intake
- a future matrix or unsupported-ledger pass adds grounded adjacent-domain detail that changes ranking precision

## Forbidden shortcuts
- Do not solve missing parity truth by writing confident prose.
- Do not hide uncertainty in milestone names.
- Do not equate internal pilot distribution with public-ready release.
- Do not treat GE-08's existence as proof that contributor workflow is settled.
- Do not turn a pilot-bounded matrix into a fake all-Pathfinder roadmap.
